# Toy models of superposition

## Abstract
Neural networks often represent more features than they have dimensions by storing them in superposition. We construct minimal toy models to analyze when and why superposition occurs as a function of feature sparsity and capacity.

## 1. Overcomplete Feature Geometry
Linear representations in neural networks can pack almost-orthogonal vectors into d dimensions when features are sparse. As sparsity increases, the maximum number of non-interfering features scales superlinearly with dimension.

We demonstrate that when feature activations are sparse (L0 << d), interference terms between non-orthogonal directions remain bounded below the noise threshold of downstream nonlinearities.
